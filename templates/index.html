Frequently asked

Is this legal?
Yes. Bill negotiation services have operated in the US since 2009 (Billshark, BillTrim, Rocket Money, BillCutterz). Arbiter acts as your authorized agent with explicit written permission. We're not pretending to be you — we say "I'm calling on behalf of [Your Name]." You can revoke authorization at any time.
What information do you need?
A copy of your bill (PDF or screenshot) and written authorization. We may need your account PIN or last-4 of SSN if the biller requires identity verification — that's encrypted and never stored in plaintext.
Can you mess up my account?
No. Our authorization is limited to rate reduction only. We cannot cancel service, add new services, change your plan tier, or modify your account beyond requesting a better rate. We always confirm "no new contract" before agreeing.
Do I pay anything if you don't save me money?
$0. Zip. If we can't negotiate a lower rate, you pay nothing. If we do, you pay 25% of your first-year savings, one time. That means you keep 75% of the savings, forever, for zero work.
How long does a call take?
Average call is 7-10 minutes including hold time. You don't need to be on the line — we handle everything and email you the result.
Why are you disclosing you're AI? Won't they hang up?
California (AB 2905) and several other states require AI disclosure on business calls. We also believe being transparent is better business. In our testing, reps don't refuse to negotiate with an AI assistant — reps talk to automated systems and third-party agents all day. If a rep does refuse, we escalate or call back with a human-in-the-loop.
What bills can you negotiate?
At launch: internet (Comcast/Xfinity, Spectrum, Verizon Fios, AT&T), mobile (Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T), TV (DirecTV, DISH), and home security (ADT). Coming soon: medical bills, car insurance, rent/mortgage, credit card APRs, and gym memberships.
Do you record the calls?
Yes, for verification and quality. We announce recording at the start of every call ("this call may be recorded"), which satisfies all-party-consent state laws. You can request a copy of your call recording any time.
